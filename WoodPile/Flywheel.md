# The Flywheel

The WoodPile improves through a repeating cycle rather than a sequence of disconnected fixes.

## The Cycle

1. **Observe** — notice a problem, result, inconsistency, cost, or opportunity.
2. **Separate fact from interpretation** — record what actually happened before deciding what it means.
3. **Test** — design the smallest useful comparison that can produce evidence.
4. **Decide** — choose the standard that best serves the project's real priorities.
5. **Build** — embody the decision in the appropriate tool.
6. **Use** — run the tool on real work at meaningful scale.
7. **Document** — preserve the evidence, reasoning, decision, and remaining uncertainty.
8. **Observe again** — let real use reveal the next useful question.

Each turn should leave the project easier to understand and more dependable than before.

## Why Documentation Is Part of the Mechanism

Without documentation, the wheel slips. Later sessions repeat tests, reopen settled questions, or invent explanations for decisions whose reasoning has been lost.

The [Codex](Codex.md) preserves durable knowledge. The [Duty Log](DutyLog.md) preserves the unfinished edge. Tool manuals preserve current implementation.

Documentation is therefore not an after-action report. It is one of the working parts of the flywheel.

## Limits

The flywheel does not mean perpetual redesign.

A settled decision remains settled until new evidence or a changed requirement justifies reopening it. Familiarity, novelty, or industry fashion alone are not sufficient reasons.
